CAD section export

DXF files use local Rhino XYZ coordinates in metres (Z up; the inverse of web X,Z,-Y). Original Rhino XYZ reverses the documented +995.425 m viewer elevation correction for reduced_site.3dm; Corrected NAP XYZ retains the current web elevation. The former aligns with the original uploaded source only if its placement and units have not subsequently changed. NAP is the default for historical layers. Imported local replacement models may use a different datum. Selected cuts export editable 3D polylines, closed geological cell outlines, water and forest symbols, Natura 2000 spans, dimensions and labels; historical comparison curves remain inferred. Overview can export all displayed section guides. No flattened drawing or viewport offset is applied to DXF geometry. SVG remains a separate, flat presentation drawing. DXF may be saved as DWG in Rhino or AutoCAD.

Luikerweg viewing platform and stairs

Built 2015, design Rademacher & de Vries, engineering Castermans. Nationale Staalprijs project record documents two 20 m steel girders (18 m exposed), variable depth 0.5–1.9 m, and approximately 40 m descent. Castermans project description documents the approximately 3 m spacing and concrete-to-steel stair transition. The visitor information describes 215 steps. The simplified web mesh uses the supplied photographs and the cached OpenStreetMap ways 860240119, 860240121, 860240123, 724705593 and 389304946 (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Heights are fitted to the terrain at the upper connection and lower reference where plausible; otherwise a 40 m drop is assumed. Tread count, widths, railing and landing geometry are approximate, not construction or surveying data. The underlying quarry mesh is unchanged, and cave openings and buried connections are not reconstructed. Toggle and original-Rhino-coordinate OBJ download in Access.

Quarry terrain, ecology and groundwater (RWS 2024)

Witteveen+Bos / Rijkswaterstaat, Technische variantenstudie laagwaterbuffer ENCI-groeve, definitive report 12 July 2024, 140081/24-010.204, with RWS memo 18 September 2024. References: Fig. 2.5 (report p. 12), Fig. 5.1 (p. 28), Fig. 5.2 (p. 30), Fig. 7.1 (p. 51), Table 2.3 and Figs. 2.8–2.10 (pp. 14–17), Fig. 4.1 (p. 24). Original figures are not redistributed. The report is a technical scenario study, not an adopted project.

Elevation bands are rendered directly from the present mesh world elevation; not the 2019 survey or original report map. Ecological elevation screening displays only the reported altitude bands for potential calcareous marsh and moist grassland, with an analyst-selected ±1 m band around +38 m. No actual ecological management or restoration polygons are asserted; the management and nature-type maps differ. Obtain the original georeferenced GIS layers and publication permission for exact spatial zoning. Water scenario levels +14.5, +18, +36 and +44 m NAP: four-neighbour connected low ground seeded by the largest mapped permanent-water component (ESA WorldCover 2020) in the approximate quarry mask and its immediate shoreline; approximate analyst-defined basin mask, 0.15 m numerical tolerance, no groundwater model, pumping simulation, bathymetry or flood forecast. Measured elevations and scenario areas may differ from the report owing to the surface model and basin limits. If the current surface conceals the basin floor, apparent coverage can be incorrect. Section lines show only the same approximate connected cells and are not included in CAD exports. Historical observation-point data are no longer displayed in the viewer. The water footprint is a 2020 land-cover classification, not a measurement of the current 2024 target level. If no terrain cell at the selected threshold adjoins the mapped lake, the viewer retains the observed footprint and reports no modelled expansion.

Factory mesh cleanup is display-side and restricted to an approximate industrial precinct: near-coincident vertices (1.5 cm local-world tolerance), duplicate and degenerate triangles removed; untextured mesh normals preserve angular creases. Original assets, surveyed geometry extents, material categories and real sawtooth roofs are retained; no wholesale simplification or replacement by boxes. Access toggle restores original rendering.

Geology / subsurface — research references

Existing terrain and environmental rasters do not contain measured geological strata. TNO – Geological Survey of the Netherlands provides the Digital Geological Model (DGM), cross-section viewer and model downloads. Geological formations relevant to ENCI include the Maastricht Formation and underlying Gulpen Formation. Site-specific research: Stratigraphy of the type Maastrichtian – a synthesis and KU Leuven Maastrichtian Geoheritage Project (georeferenced quarry models and reference samples). DGM and the research team’s georeferenced model have not been imported. The earlier simplified section was interpreted from ARCADIS / ENCI B.V., Kalksteenwinning ENCI tot 2020 — Startnotitie, 14 December 2007, pp. 9–10, §2.3, Fig. 2.3. The report gives approximately +90 to +45 m NAP for Maastricht Formation and +45 to −35 m NAP for Gulpen Formation. The earlier version modelled these contacts as horizontal and clips them to a terrain-sampled section within the study perimeter. Upper material, quarry spoil, backfill and submerged ground are not independently identified. Member sequence references Jagt & Jagt-Yazykova (2012; CC BY 4.0), not assigned to absolute elevations. No source figure has been reproduced. Regional geological models are not a substitute for local rock-mass and geotechnical surveys.

Additional evidence: TNO Geological Survey — Sint-Pietersberg describes Oligocene fine sand, Meuse gravel/sand and locally loess over the limestone. The 2007 ARCADIS report (p. 9) describes original overburden of roughly 15 m and partial deposition at D’n Observant. Its current geometry is not established. BRO DGM v2.2.1 (2025) is a regional borehole-derived model; download instructions. No DGM grid or borehole data have been imported yet. Section reference bands are illustrative, not local geological contacts; do not infer rock mass capacity from them.

Cliff selection uses the uploaded AHN-derived Slope and Aspect classified rasters (10 m); threshold class ≥45°, aspect bins 45°, representative cut centers at least 120 m apart (maximum 24); 2 m samples from the simplified uploaded 2026 mesh. Historical profiles remain reconstructions. A slope pixel is not proof of exposed rock.

Follow-up site-specific research: Witteveen+Bos / Rijkswaterstaat, Technische variantenstudie laagwaterbuffer ENCI-groeve (12 July 2024), section 2.3 (geohydrology and soil structure), section 7.3.11 (bank and wall instability). The uploaded final report (12 July 2024), §2.3, pp. 12–13, Fig. 2.6, uses BRO REGIS II v2.2.1. It describes Vaals ~−80 to −45 m NAP, Gulpen ~−45 to +45 m, Maastricht ~+45 to +75 m in the regional section and the quarried Maastricht interval up to +90 m. The atlas now uses these as schematic horizontal reference bands and hatches +75 to +90 m as uncertain, not as a separate formation. Actual contacts vary along the original regional transect. Original overburden was ~15 m (loess/sand/gravel), partly deposited at D’n Observant; current fill remains unmapped. Section 7.3.11 (p. 57) identifies rapid water drawdown and wave erosion as potential rock-wall instability mechanisms in proposed water-buffer scenarios, not a local stability survey. The 2024 report indicates that, at the time, only Oehoevallei within the quarry itself was designated Natura 2000 (§2.1.3); consult the current official geometry. The original report figure is not reproduced. Regional DGM grids (100 × 100 m) are available through PDOK download; local boreholes and as-built reclamation records are needed to identify anthropogenic fill.
