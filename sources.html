DGM grid / local model import

TNO Geological Survey of the Netherlands, DGM v02r2, map sheet 69w. The optional cropped JSON contains original 100 m grid-cell top and base elevations in RD New / m NAP. The architectural section and 3D cut face use native cell values without interpolation, clipped to the uploaded terrain along the selected section segment. Cliff candidate centres are inside the approximate study area; the full transect may extend beyond it. The site grid is referenced by an RD anchor; alignment remains approximate. Empty cells are not inferred. Modelled strata are not borehole observations or a rock-mass survey; present anthropogenic fill is not verified. DGM file loaded locally stays in the browser session; publishing it requires the repository snapshot and verification of data reuse terms. TNO DGM source.

Geology / subsurface — research references

Existing terrain and environmental rasters do not contain measured geological strata. TNO – Geological Survey of the Netherlands provides the Digital Geological Model (DGM), cross-section viewer and model downloads. Geological formations relevant to ENCI include the Maastricht Formation and underlying Gulpen Formation. Site-specific research: Stratigraphy of the type Maastrichtian – a synthesis and KU Leuven Maastrichtian Geoheritage Project (georeferenced quarry models and reference samples). DGM and the research team’s georeferenced model have not been imported. The earlier simplified section was interpreted from ARCADIS / ENCI B.V., Kalksteenwinning ENCI tot 2020 — Startnotitie, 14 December 2007, pp. 9–10, §2.3, Fig. 2.3. The report gives approximately +90 to +45 m NAP for Maastricht Formation and +45 to −35 m NAP for Gulpen Formation. The earlier version modelled these contacts as horizontal and clips them to a terrain-sampled section within the study perimeter. Upper material, quarry spoil, backfill and submerged ground are not independently identified. Member sequence references Jagt & Jagt-Yazykova (2012; CC BY 4.0), not assigned to absolute elevations. No source figure has been reproduced. Regional geological models are not a substitute for local rock-mass and geotechnical surveys.

Additional evidence: TNO Geological Survey — Sint-Pietersberg describes Oligocene fine sand, Meuse gravel/sand and locally loess over the limestone. The 2007 ARCADIS report (p. 9) describes original overburden of roughly 15 m and partial deposition at D’n Observant. Its current geometry is not established. BRO DGM v2.2.1 (2025) is a regional borehole-derived model; download instructions. No DGM grid or borehole data have been imported yet. Section reference bands are illustrative, not local geological contacts; do not infer rock mass capacity from them.

Cliff selection uses the uploaded AHN-derived Slope and Aspect classified rasters (10 m); threshold class ≥45°, aspect bins 45°, representative cut centers at least 120 m apart (maximum 24); 2 m samples from the simplified uploaded 2026 mesh. Historical profiles remain reconstructions. A slope pixel is not proof of exposed rock.

Follow-up site-specific research: Witteveen+Bos / Rijkswaterstaat, Technische variantenstudie laagwaterbuffer ENCI-groeve (12 July 2024), section 2.3 (geohydrology and soil structure), section 7.3.11 (bank and wall instability). The uploaded final report (12 July 2024), §2.3, pp. 12–13, Fig. 2.6, uses BRO REGIS II v2.2.1. It describes Vaals ~−80 to −45 m NAP, Gulpen ~−45 to +45 m, Maastricht ~+45 to +75 m in the regional section and the quarried Maastricht interval up to +90 m. The atlas now uses these as schematic horizontal reference bands and hatches +75 to +90 m as uncertain, not as a separate formation. Actual contacts vary along the original regional transect. Original overburden was ~15 m (loess/sand/gravel), partly deposited at D’n Observant; current fill remains unmapped. Section 7.3.11 (p. 57) identifies rapid water drawdown and wave erosion as potential rock-wall instability mechanisms in proposed water-buffer scenarios, not a local stability survey. The 2024 report indicates that, at the time, only Oehoevallei within the quarry itself was designated Natura 2000 (§2.1.3); consult the current official geometry. The original report figure is not reproduced. Regional DGM grids (100 × 100 m) are available through PDOK download; local boreholes and as-built reclamation records are needed to identify anthropogenic fill.

Walking / access

  • © OpenStreetMap contributors, Open Database License (ODbL). OSM highway way geometry: vehicle and service roads extend to the full terrain rectangle; mapped walking/cycling paths and steps remain within the approximate ENCI study perimeter plus 150 m. Roads exclude private/no-access ways and minor parking aisles/driveways. Data are simplified and clipped; source coverage and local georeference are approximate. Roads, paths and steps are display categories; no regional hiking relations or exact official Natuurmonumenten itineraries are plotted. The browser prefers the published site-access-full.json snapshot, falling back to the older local-only site-access.json if needed. The separate GitHub cache workflow prepares the data from OSM; no live map calls are made by the viewer. Not a legal or accessibility survey. OpenStreetMap may not reflect current restrictions.
  • Natuurmonumenten — Sint-Pietersberg. Official route names, distances and links are references; no website photos or route descriptions reproduced. GPX files imported by the visitor remain in the local browser session. Permission for public redistribution has not been verified.
